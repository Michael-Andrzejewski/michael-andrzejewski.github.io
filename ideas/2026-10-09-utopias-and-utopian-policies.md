# Over 300 utopias, from dath ilan to Mondragon

The research notes hold well over 300 distinct utopias and concrete utopian policies. The seven focus figures alone supply about 170 of them. Most come from two figures: Eliezer Yudkowsky's fictional dath ilan (34 named institutions, such as a land value tax paid out to citizens, prediction markets, universal cryonics and a secret AI-safety project) and Seb Krier's agent economy (about 39 mechanisms, built on one idea: give every person an AI agent that negotiates for them, so cheap bargaining can replace blunt bans). Dario Amodei gives the most concrete near-term list (a 3% tax on AI usage, mandatory gene synthesis screening, a doubled lifespan, 20% growth in poor countries). Isaac Arthur gives the most careful definitions (post-scarcity as five conditions, money that survives abundance, habitats people can leave). Gwern and Janus focus on the individual and on AI minds: a personal AI trained on one person, writing so future AIs carry your values, and preserving retired models as heritage. For Vie McCoy the notes found an identity and themes but no concrete policies. Across the wider field, three findings stand out. First, the newest work (2025 to 2026) has moved away from fixed blueprints toward "keep options open" designs. Second, the real-world ideas that last are narrow institutions, such as a resource fund with a payout rule or a worker co-op charter, while whole-society experiments tend to collapse or be absorbed by a state. Third, many famous "utopias" are really warnings. Each item below carries a label so the reader can tell a proposal from a story or a warning.

**How to read the labels.** **ADVOCATED** means the person argues for it in their own voice. **EXPLORED** means they present it as possible without endorsing it. **FICTION** means it appears in a story or invented world; the author may like it but does not offer it as a plan. **CAUTIONARY** means it was built to show something going wrong, or offered only as a necessary evil. **FORECAST** means a prediction rather than a wish. **UNREAD** means the researchers saw only a title or short description, so the stance is unknown. **REAL** means it was enacted or tried. Where the notes flag a source as secondhand or unverified, the entry says so.

## The seven focus figures supply about 170 items

### 1. Isaac Arthur: abundance has limits, and exit keeps utopias honest

Isaac Arthur runs the YouTube channel Science & Futurism with Isaac Arthur. He maps which future societies could work rather than prescribing one. His own words: he tries "to figure out how they could not operate, and assume that anything inside those specific bounds is possible" ([transcript](https://github.com/CuteLifeBot/IsaacArthur/blob/master/vids/Post-Scarcity-Civilizations.md)). Where he does commit, his commitments are these. Money and a little coercion survive abundance. Honest information is a core good. Radical changes (hive minds, implants, life extension) must be voluntary. AIs deserve personhood. Unused stars are wasted energy. **Caveat:** mechanism detail comes from community transcripts covering 2015 to March 2018 only. Later episodes are known from titles and descriptions, so they are marked UNREAD.

### 2. Eliezer Yudkowsky: dath ilan is the richest single store of institutions

Yudkowsky's material comes in four layers, and the labels matter. **dath ilan** is FICTION: a parallel Earth with far better coordination. It started as an April Fools post ([Tumblr, 2014](https://yudkowsky.tumblr.com/post/81447230971/my-april-fools-day-confession)) and grew through a 2021 question-and-answer session ([LessWrong AMA](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/gvA4j8pGYG4xtaTkw/i-m-from-a-parallel-earth-with-much-higher-coordination-ama)) and the long collaborative story *Project Lawful / planecrash* ([glowfic](https://www.glowfic.com/posts/4582)). He calls it "rational fiction", meaning events he thinks would realistically follow. Many details are clearly ideas he likes, but he never endorsed it as a blueprint, and some details are jokes. **Fun Theory** and **CEV** are ADVOCATED theory. **Failed Utopia #4-2** and **Three Worlds Collide** are CAUTIONARY. His **AI treaty** work is ADVOCATED present-day policy.

### 3. Seb Krier: an agent for everyone turns rules into negotiations

Krier works in policy at Google DeepMind and is a senior fellow at the Cosmos Institute; he writes in a personal capacity. His flagship idea is "Coasean bargaining at scale". The economist Ronald Coase showed that if bargaining were free, people could settle disputes like noise or pollution by paying each other, without bans. Krier argues AI agents make bargaining nearly free. Every person gets a "fiduciary" agent, meaning one legally bound to serve that person's interests, and the state shrinks to a "framework guarantor" (property, contracts, courts, bans on catastrophic risks). Everything below is ADVOCATED unless marked.

## Classic and science-fiction utopias keep reusing a few levers

Pre-modern utopias are mostly blueprints. They abolish or cap property, shorten working hours (More 6 hours a day, Campanella 4, Skinner 4, Callenbach 20 a week) and equalise consumption through storehouses or credits. From Morris onward, writers distrust the central planner. Modern science fiction assumes abundance and asks who decides. The answers are a superintelligent steward, a choice among many polities, or commons plus local democracy. **Caveat:** these descriptions come from standard reference pages not re-fetched this session; spot-check exact numbers.

The pattern holds across the table. New Harmony collapsed, kibbutzim privatised, Christiania normalised, Rojava was absorbed, and Próspera is fighting in court. The Alaska fund, Mondragon, Vienna's housing and Ireland's assemblies endure because each is one mechanism plugged into an existing state. Cash pilots answer the "people will stop working" objection fairly well. None has tested a permanent, fully universal basic income.

## Conclusion

The catalogue shows the same few mechanisms recurring. Land rent shared with everyone appears in Paine, George, dath ilan, Altman and Alaska. Exit plus many small polities appears in Nozick, Scott Alexander's archipelago, Arthur's departing habitats, Palmer's Hives and dath ilan's regions. Cheaper coordination appears in Krier's agents, futarchy, quadratic funding and Polis. Where the focus figures add something new, it is in two places. The first is treating AI as a coordination technology that makes old mechanism-design ideas workable at street level (Krier, Amodei's consensus tools). The second is treating AI systems as stakeholders whose welfare and continuity count (Arthur's personhood stance, Janus and Antra's model preservation, Krier's "sophonts", Bostrom and Shulman). The real-world record says the second half of any utopian plan matters most. Ideas survive as narrow, auditable institutions inside a host state, not as whole new societies.

The biggest open tension is secrecy and control against openness and choice. dath ilan, the Vulnerable World panopticon and AI 2027's committee all buy safety by concentrating knowledge or power. Viatopia, Arthur's honesty test and Krier's framework state try to keep it spread out. Yudkowsky and Amodei now both propose slowing AI on purpose, Yudkowsky by treaty and Amodei by "pacing" (that essay is not yet verified on his site). The most practical near-term utopian policy may therefore be the boring one: buy time with compute monitoring, and pilot the coordination tools in small towns first, as Krier suggests. The open gaps to check are Isaac Arthur's post-2018 episodes, Janus's X posts, any readable essay by Vie McCoy, and Gwern's older essays.
